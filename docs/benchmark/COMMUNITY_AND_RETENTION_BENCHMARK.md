# Community and Retention Benchmark

## Useful patterns

- Saves, meal plans, shopping lists, and cooking history make a recipe service useful after the first visit.
- Community signals can create trust only when their provenance, recency, and moderation are real.
- Newsletter prompts work best after useful content engagement, not as a blocking gate.

## House Chef decision

Existing favourites, pantry, cooking sessions, matching, personalization foundation, and shopping/meal-planning foundations should be made reliable and understandable before opening community features.

## Sequenced opportunities

1. **P1:** user-owned collections, saved filters, and recently viewed controls with retention limits.
2. **P1:** dependable shopping-list merge/scaling/print flow and weekly plan explanation.
3. **P2:** newsletter preferences and consent-aware post-save CTA.
4. **P3:** ratings, comments, cooking results, and user photos only after moderation, reports, anti-spam, audit, user blocking, and explicit policy are live.

## Non-negotiables

No seeded reviews, fake chef profiles, fabricated ratings, or auto-published user submissions.
